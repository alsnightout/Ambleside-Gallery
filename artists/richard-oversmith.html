THE ARTIST

The poetry
of place
and light.

Richard Oversmith is an American impressionist painter whose work reflects a lifelong fascination with the relationship between light, atmosphere, and place. Working primarily in oil on linen, he captures the character of landscapes through expressive brushwork and a sensitive understanding of color.

Oversmith received his formal training in fine art and illustration at Kendall College of Art and Design in Grand Rapids, Michigan.

During his studies, he was invited to attend the Royal College of Art in London, England, where he developed his experience in plein air painting and discovered a clearer direction for his work as an oil painter.

After graduating from Kendall College with a Bachelor of Fine Arts degree in 1995, Oversmith returned to his home state of North Carolina to pursue a professional career in painting.

The natural beauty of Western North Carolina has remained a constant source of inspiration. From the rolling Blue Ridge Mountains and abundant waterfalls to historic estates and quiet farm scenes, the region offers an endless variety of subjects.

His artistic travels have taken him throughout the United States and overseas to France and Italy. Whether working close to home or abroad, Oversmith immerses himself in the landscape, painting directly from observation in the plein air tradition.

His work extends beyond landscapes to still life compositions, where he explores subjects ranging from the classical to the imaginative.

THE PRACTICE

Painting
the moment.

For Richard Oversmith, painting is an invitation for the viewer to engage with the scene. Rather than attempting to reproduce every detail, he searches for the essential visual relationships that communicate a sense of place.

His paintings are shaped by the interaction of color, value, edges, and drawing. Each brushstroke contributes to the atmosphere and mood of the finished composition.

Working in oil on linen, Oversmith embraces a medium that allows him to express both the immediacy of direct observation and the richness of his artistic interpretation.

His approach has developed through a lifelong study of historical masters, the influence of contemporary painters, and his own evolving artistic instincts.

In the plein air tradition, he allows the setting to guide his work, using only as many brushstrokes as necessary to communicate the scene's essential character.
