Noé Katz

Painting · Sculpture

Noé Katz

THE ARTIST

Imagination
made visible.

Noé Katz was born in Mexico City and grew up near Parque México, surrounded by the movement, color and folklore of urban life. Street vendors, craftspeople, balloons, food and the constant activity of the city helped shape his imagination from an early age.

His family background gave him an equally broad cultural perspective. His father emigrated from Europe during the Second World War, while his mother was born in Sonora, Mexico. Katz has described this European and Mexican inheritance as giving him an international way of seeing the world.

As his interest in art developed, Katz became deeply engaged with European modernism and the work of artists including Pablo Picasso, Paul Klee and Giorgio de Chirico. He was especially struck by Picasso's freedom, fluency of line and ability to transform imagination into visual form.

For Katz, imagination remains central to artistic life. He sees it as something rooted in childhood and something that must be protected and continually developed throughout an artist's life.

ARTIST'S VISION

“What matters in art is that it is original, that it is yours, and that it creates wonder.”

— Noé Katz

FORMATION

Mexico,
Florence and
the Renaissance.

Katz won an award through the Italian Embassy in Mexico that allowed him to study in Florence, where he lived for three years.

He has described Florence as a magical place, saturated with art and with the energy of students and artists from around the world. The Renaissance became especially important to his development, along with the medieval and early Renaissance traditions that preceded it.

After returning to Mexico, Katz studied graphic design. He credits design with teaching him discipline: how to develop an idea, carry it through and complete it. At the same time, he distinguishes fine art by the freedom it gives the artist to decide what a work must become.

THE WORK

Figuration,
color and
sculptural form.

Katz has continued primarily through painting and painted sculpture. He describes a practice in which the language of painting is carried into sculptural form, combining figuration, color and three-dimensional structure.

He has said that he prefers to work through figuration rather than abstraction and has developed an approach based on softened transitions, moving color and relatively restrained surface texture.

For Katz, originality is not a matter of style alone. It comes from developing a personal world — one shaped by memory, travel, history, craft, observation and imagination.

PHILOSOPHY

Art & Wonder

IMAGINATION

Katz describes imagination as the foundation of creativity and as something artists must preserve from childhood onward.

ORIGINALITY

He emphasizes the importance of developing a world that is genuinely one's own rather than simply repeating familiar artistic forms.

WONDER

Katz believes powerful art produces asombro — a sense of astonishment or wonder in the viewer.

HUMANITY

He describes art as something that humanizes us and connects painting, sculpture, architecture, poetry, cinema, theater and other creative forms through imagination.
